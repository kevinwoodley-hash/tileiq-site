How much tile adhesive do I need per m² and per 20kg bag?

Updated 5 November 2026 · 6 min read · By Kevin, City & Guilds Level 2 qualified tiler

Quick answer

Allow about 2.5–3 kg per m² for standard tiles, 2 kg per m² for mosaics and small tiles, and about 5 kg per m² for large-format tiles and external floors (Mapei Adesilex P9 data sheet).

That means a 20kg bag covers roughly 6–7.5 m² with a 6mm notched trowel and 4–5 m² with a 10mm trowel. On walls with small tiles a bag can stretch to 8–10 m². Uneven surfaces and back-buttering use more, so round up.

There's no single answer, because coverage depends on the trowel, the tile and what you're sticking it to. But the manufacturers publish figures, and they're close enough to plan a job and order the right number of bags.

Walls vs floors

Walls usually take less adhesive than floors, because wall tiles are smaller and lighter and go on with a smaller notch. As an example, a UK retailer listing for BAL Flex One gives these approximate figures for one 20kg bag:

WhereOne 20kg bag coversWorks out at
Dry wallabout 10 m²about 2 kg/m²
Wet wallabout 8 m²about 2.5 kg/m²
Floorabout 5.3 m²about 3.8 kg/m²
 Source: Craven Dunnill listing for BAL Flex One 20kg. The kg/m² column is the bag weight divided by the area.

Wet walls take more because you want full coverage behind every tile, with no voids for water to sit in. If you're measuring walls from scratch, the wall tile calculator takes off doors and windows and works out the adhesive too.

Large-format tiles

Large-format tiles need a thicker, fuller bed, and usually back-buttering too. Mapei gives large-format adhesive use by bed thickness: about 1.2 kg per m² for every mm of bed.

What makes a bag go further or run out sooner

  • Uneven walls and floors soak up far more adhesive. Level or board out first. It's usually cheaper than filling hollows with adhesive.
  • Back-buttering large or heavy tiles adds roughly another millimetre of bed.
  • Textured or ribbed tile backs take more to fill.
  • Solid bed for showers and external floors uses more than a standard notched bed.
  • Mixing more than you can use before it goes off. Rapid-set adhesives give you less working time.

Lift a tile early on to check you're getting full coverage. If you're not, go up a trowel size rather than pressing harder.

Worked examples

Each example uses the data sheet figure, then rounds up. Exact multiples of a bag leave nothing spare, so treat them as the minimum.

Bathroom walls

20 m² of 300×600mm wall tiles in a shower area, at about 2.5–3 kg/m²: 20 × 2.5 to 20 × 3 = 50–60 kg. Three 20kg bags cover it only if everything goes to plan. Add 10% for out-of-true walls and the odd remix and it's up to 66 kg, so plan for 4 bags. Pricing the whole bathroom? See how to quote a bathroom tiling job.

Kitchen floor

12 m² of 600×600mm porcelain with a 10mm trowel, at about 5 kg/m²: 12 × 5 = 60 kg, exactly 3 bags with nothing spare. If you're back-buttering or the floor isn't flat, plan for 4 bags. The guide to quoting a kitchen floor covers the rest of the job.

Large-format floor

8 m² of 1200×600mm porcelain, at Mapei's 5 kg/m² for large sizes and floors: 8 × 5 = 40 kg, exactly 2 bags. Large tiles nearly always need back-buttering for full coverage, so plan for 3 bags.

For a whole job, the free tile calculator works out adhesive, grout and tiles together. The floor and wall calculators work it out from the room size. For wastage, grout and trims, see how much tile and materials to order.

Common questions

How much tile adhesive do I need for 10 m²?

For normal size tiles at 2.5–3 kg/m², about 25–30 kg, which is two 20kg bags. Allow a third if the surface is uneven or you're back-buttering. Mosaics need about 20 kg and large tiles about 50 kg.

Do floors need more adhesive than walls?

Usually, yes. Floor tiles are bigger and go down with a larger notch, so expect around 4–5 kg/m² on floors against 2–3 kg/m² on walls.

Does back-buttering use a lot more adhesive?

It adds roughly another millimetre of bed across the tile. On large tiles that's worth an extra bag on most floors.

Can I use these figures for ready-mixed tub adhesive?

No. Ready-mixed tubs are a different product, and their coverage is printed on the tub. Use that figure, or enter it as a custom rate in the calculator.

Why does the calculator give a range instead of an exact amount?

Manufacturers publish a range for normal tiles, and real use moves with the surface, the trowel and how you lay. The range shows what to expect; the bag count rounds up from the top of it so you don't run short.

Coverage figures are manufacturer and retailer guides (see Sources). They change with the product, trowel, tile and surface. Always check the technical data sheet for the adhesive you're using.

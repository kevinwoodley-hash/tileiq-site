Tiling prices

How much does a tiler charge in the UK? (2026)

Updated 5 November 2026 · 5 min read · By Kevin, City & Guilds Level 2 qualified tiler

Quick answer

Most UK tilers charge around £50 per m² for labour, a day rate of £200–£350 (about £300 is typical) or around £40 an hour. Expect more in London, for small rooms with a lot of cutting, and for porcelain, natural stone, mosaic or large-format tiles.

Adhesive and grout add about £10 per m², and the tiles are extra.

There's no fixed rate for tiling labour in the UK. What a tiler charges depends on where they work, the tile, the surface and how much prep the job needs. The published price guides agree on the general level, though. If you're a tiler, it shows where your own rate sits. If you're having a room tiled, it shows what a fair quote looks like.

Bathrooms often cost more than the per m² rate suggests. They need prep, tanking around the shower and a lot of cutting around fittings. See how to quote a bathroom tiling job for a full worked example, and how much to charge to tile a shower.

What makes a tiler charge more

  • Where you are. Tilers in London charge more than those in the North and much of the rest of the UK (MyBuilder).
  • The tile. Ceramic is the cheapest to fit. Porcelain, mosaic, natural stone and glass cost more (MyBuilder).
  • Large-format tiles. They're slower to lay than normal tiles (Checkatrade) and need a flatter surface, more adhesive and often two people to handle.
  • Small or fiddly areas. Below about 10 m², the price per m² often goes up or there's a small surcharge (Checkatrade).
  • Prep. Removing old tiles, levelling, boarding and tanking are usually priced separately from laying.

How much a tiler can lay in a day

Checkatrade's guide gives typical output of 10–15 m² a day on walls and 8–12 m² a day on floors. Use this to check that a per m² rate and a day rate agree with each other:

  • 12 m² a day at £25/m² is £300 a day.
  • 10 m² a day at £35/m² is £350 a day.

If a per m² rate works out well below the day rate on small or fiddly jobs, those jobs need a minimum charge or a day rate instead.

Many tilers quote per m² for the main areas and use a day rate, or a fixed sum, for prep and anything uncertain. Whichever you use, check one against the other. The tiling labour calculator for tilers shows what a per m² price earns per day.

What's included in a labour rate

A quote should say clearly what the rate covers. Customers compare quotes line by line, and a lower price that leaves out prep isn't really cheaper. Things that are often priced separately:

  • removing old tiles and making good the wall or floor;
  • levelling compound or screed on uneven floors;
  • tanking in showers and wet rooms;
  • backer board or plasterboard;
  • trims, silicone sealing and boxing in pipes;
  • waste removal.

Setting your own rate

Common questions

How much does a tiler charge per hour?

Around £40 an hour (Checkatrade, MyBuilder), though Checkatrade's table also lists £50 an hour for wall tiling. Most tilers only charge by the hour for small repairs; bigger jobs are priced per m² or by the day.

How much does a tiler charge per day?

Usually £200–£350, with about £300 typical (Checkatrade). Tilers in London usually charge more (MyBuilder).

How much does a tiler charge per m²?

Around £50 per m² for labour on average (Checkatrade, MyBuilder). Adhesive and grout add about £10 per m², and the tiles are extra.

How much does it cost to tile a bathroom?

MyBuilder's average for the floor and walls is £500–£1,200, over three to four days, but its prices are for 2024 and bathrooms with tanking and a lot of cutting cost more. A tiler should break the quote down into labour, prep, tanking and materials.

Do tilers charge a minimum?

Often, yes. On areas under about 10 m², expect a higher rate per m² or a small surcharge (Checkatrade), because setting up and cutting take as long on a small job as on a big one.

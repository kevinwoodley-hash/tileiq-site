Tiling prices

Tiling labour rates in the UK (2026)

Updated 20 October 2026 · 3 min read · By the TileIQ team

Quick answer

Most UK tilers charge £20–£50 per m² for labour, or a day rate of £200–£350 (around £300 is typical). Expect the top of the range in London and the South East, and for large-format, natural stone, mosaic or herringbone work.

Labour, adhesive and grout together usually come to £80–£130 per m², before the cost of the tiles.

There's no fixed rate for tiling. What you can charge depends on where you work, the tile, the surface and how much prep the job needs. The published price guides do agree on the general range, though, and it's useful to know where your own rate sits within it.

Rates by region

Labour rates follow local living costs and demand. MyBuilder's 2026 guide gives these per m² labour ranges:

RegionLabour per m²
London and the South East£30–£40
Midlands and the North£20–£30
Scotland and Wales£18–£35

These are averages across many jobs. Experienced tilers with a full diary often charge above them, especially for bathrooms, where the job includes prep, tanking and finishing work as well as laying tiles.

Rates by tile type

Harder-to-handle tiles take longer to cut and lay, so they cost more per m². MyBuilder's figures for labour, adhesive and grout combined (tiles not included):

TileFitted, per m² (excl. tiles)Why it costs more or less
Ceramic£60–£95Light, easy to cut
Porcelain£70–£110Harder to cut, heavier, needs a better adhesive
Natural stone£80–£120Sealing, variation in thickness, careful handling
Mosaic£90–£140Slow to set out and grout

Large-format tiles (600×1200 mm and bigger) usually sit at the top of the labour range. They need a flatter substrate, more adhesive, levelling clips and often two people to handle.

How much a tiler can lay in a day

Checkatrade's guide gives typical output of 10–15 m² a day on walls and 8–12 m² a day on floors. Use this to check that your per m² rate and day rate agree with each other:

  • 12 m² a day at £25/m² is £300 a day.
  • 10 m² a day at £35/m² is £350 a day.

If your per m² rate works out well below your day rate on small or fiddly jobs, those jobs need a minimum charge or a day rate instead. Checkatrade notes that areas under about 10 m² often carry one.

What's included in a labour rate

Say clearly in your quote what the rate covers. Customers compare quotes line by line, and a lower price that leaves out prep isn't really cheaper. Things that are often priced separately:

  • removing old tiles and making good the wall or floor;
  • levelling compound or screed on uneven floors;
  • tanking in showers and wet rooms;
  • backer board or plasterboard;
  • trims, silicone sealing and boxing in pipes;
  • waste removal.

Setting your own rate

Published averages are a starting point. Work your rate out from what you need to earn and how much you lay. Our guide to working out a self-employed tiler's day rate takes you through it step by step, and how to quote a bathroom tiling job shows the rate used in a real quote.

How to manage tiling jobs and customers

Updated 20 October 2026 · 2 min read · By the TileIQ team

Quick answer

Give every job a clear stage, from enquiry through quoted, accepted, booked and in progress to complete and paid, and keep it in one place rather than spread across texts, notebooks and spreadsheets.

Then do three things every week: follow up on quotes you haven't heard back on, book in jobs that have been accepted, and chase unpaid invoices.

Most tilers are good at the tiling and struggle with everything else. Enquiries come in by text, WhatsApp, Facebook and phone. Quotes get written in the evening. Dates get pencilled in a diary, and invoices get sent whenever there's time. When work is quiet you can keep it all in your head. When it's busy, things get missed.

Give every job a stage

StageWhat needs to happen
EnquiryReply quickly, get photos and rough sizes, book a visit if needed
MeasuredRoom sizes, tile choice, prep needed, photos of the job
QuotedQuote sent. Follow up if you haven't heard back after a few days.
AcceptedAgree a start date, take a deposit if you use them, order materials
Booked / in progressConfirm the start date with the customer and keep them updated
CompleteInvoice the same day. Ask for a review a few days later.
PaidRecord the payment. Add photos to your portfolio.

Knowing the stage of every job tells you what to do next. The jobs that cost you money are the ones stuck in the middle: quotes nobody chased, accepted jobs never booked, finished jobs never invoiced.

Keep customer details in one place

For each customer keep the name, address, phone, email, what was quoted and any agreed changes. Customers often come back, or recommend you, years later, and it helps to know exactly which tiles and grout you used. Store their details securely. Under UK GDPR you're responsible for the customer data you hold.

A weekly 20-minute routine

  1. Chase quotes sent more than a few days ago with no reply. A short, polite message wins a surprising number of jobs.
  2. Book in accepted jobs that don't have a start date yet.
  3. Check next week's diary: materials ordered, customers told when you're arriving.
  4. Chase overdue invoices.
  5. Add finished work to your portfolio while the photos are on your phone.

Notebook and diaryVery few jobs, no learning curveYou need to find an old quote, or you lose the book
SpreadsheetCheap, flexibleOn site, on a phone, and quotes and invoices are separate documents

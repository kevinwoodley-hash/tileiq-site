Worked example: tiling a bathroom floor to ceiling

A bathroom with walls of 2.4, 1.8, 2.4 and 1.8 m, tiled to a height of 2.4 m. There's one door (about 1.6 m²) and a 0.6 × 0.9 m window (0.54 m²). The tiles are 600 × 300 mm, 9 mm thick, laid straight with 3 mm joints, in boxes of 1.08 m².

Window reveals aren't in that figure. Measure their depth around the frame and add them as extra area: they're small, but every piece is a cut.

Measuring walls for tiles

  • Behind a bath: start the tiled height from the top of the bath, not the floor, and measure the bath panel separately if you're tiling it.
  • Splashbacks and half-height tiling: use the height you're actually tiling to, such as the top of the splashback or the dado line.
  • Boxing and pipe chases: add the faces of the boxing as extra area.
  • Showers: the shower walls need tanking before tiling. Price it as its own line: see pricing tanking.

Adhesive for wall tiles

Mapei's data sheet gives about 2.5–3 kg per m² for standard wall tiles and about 2 kg per m² for mosaics. Large-format tiles on walls need more, about 5 kg per m², and may need back-buttering. Check the adhesive is rated for the tile's weight and the background, especially on plasterboard. See how much adhesive per m² for wall tiles, including wet walls.

Frequently asked questions

How many wall tiles do I need?

Add up the width of each wall you're tiling, multiply by the tiled height, and take off doors and windows. Add 10% wastage for a straight lay, then divide by the area of one tile, or by the m² in a box, and round up.

Do I take off doors and windows when measuring for wall tiles?

Yes. Take off each door and window, then add back the window reveals, which you'll tile. A standard internal door is about 0.8 m wide and 2 m high, or roughly 1.6 m².

How much adhesive do I need for wall tiles?

About 2.5–3 kg per m² for standard wall tiles, and about 2 kg per m² for mosaics. So 18 m² of standard wall tiles at 3 kg per m² needs 54 kg, which is three 20 kg bags.

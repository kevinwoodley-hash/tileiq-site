How to price a tiling job properly.

Undercharge and you're working for nothing. Overcharge without knowing why, and you lose the job to someone who can explain their number. Here's a straightforward way to build a price you can stand behind.

Start with a rate, not a guess

Most tilers price a job one of two ways: a day rate for labour, or a price per m². Neither is "more correct" — they suit different jobs.

  • Day rate works well for smaller jobs, awkward layouts, or when the scope isn't fully fixed yet — you're selling your time.
  • Price per m² works well for larger, more predictable areas — it's easier for a customer to compare against another quote, and easier for you to scale up for bigger rooms.

Many tilers use both: a day rate to work out what a job should cost in labour, then convert that into a per-m² figure for the quote itself.

What to factor in beyond the tiles

The materials and the visible tiling work are only part of the job. A price that only covers "laying tiles" is a price that quietly loses money on everything around it.

  • Preparation — levelling compound, removing old tiles, repairing the substrate.
  • Waste and offcuts — a wastage allowance on materials, not just the tiled area.
  • Complexity — diagonal or herringbone layouts, small mosaics, and lots of cuts around fittings all take longer than a straight lay in a simple room.
  • Travel and access — distance to the job, parking, and how easy it is to get materials in.
  • Overheads — van, tools, insurance, and the time spent quoting and invoicing that isn't billed directly.

A simple worked example

20m² bathroom floor, medium complexity

Tiles + adhesive + grout (with wastage)Materials cost
Preparation — levelling, removing old tilesHalf a day's labour
Tiling — straight lay, some cuts around fittings1–1.5 days' labour
Total priceMaterials + labour at your day rate, plus margin

The exact figures depend on your day rate, your local material costs, and the specific job — the structure is what matters: materials + preparation time + tiling time + margin, not a single number pulled from memory.

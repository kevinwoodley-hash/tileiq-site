How much should a tiler charge?

There's no single correct day rate or price per m² — it depends on where you are, how experienced you are, and what the job actually involves. But there's a sensible way to work it out, instead of copying a number you saw online.

Two common ways to charge

Most UK tilers price using one of two methods, often blended together:

  • Day rate — a fixed rate for your time, regardless of exactly how much area gets covered that day. Common for smaller or more unpredictable jobs.
  • Price per m² — a rate multiplied by the area, sometimes with a different rate for walls vs floors, or for straight lay vs complex patterns. Common for larger, more predictable jobs.
Rates vary a lot by region, experience and job type — a rough national average isn't much use for pricing a specific job in your area. The better approach is working out your own numbers using the method below, then checking they're broadly in line with other local tilers.

What actually moves the price

Experience & reputation

A tiler with a strong reputation and a full diary can charge more than someone just starting out — and often should.

Region

Rates in and around major cities are typically higher than more rural areas, reflecting the local cost of living and demand.

Job complexity

Herringbone, large-format tiles, small mosaics and lots of cuts around fittings all take longer than a straight lay in a simple room.

Preparation required

Levelling an uneven floor or stripping old tiles adds real time that has to be priced, not absorbed.

Business costs tilers often forget to charge for

A day rate that only covers the hours physically laying tiles usually isn't covering the full cost of running the business around it.

  • Van, fuel and tool costs
  • Public liability insurance
  • Time spent quoting, invoicing and chasing payment
  • Slow periods where there's less work booked in
  • Training, certifications and keeping skills up to date

If none of that is built into your rate, you can be fully booked and still not making the money it looks like on paper.

A sensible way to land on your number

Work out what you need to earn in a year to cover your take-home pay and the business costs above, divide by the number of days you'll realistically be tiling (not every working day — allow for quoting, travel and quiet periods), and that's your baseline day rate. From there, convert it to a per-m² rate for typical job sizes, and adjust up for complexity or down for very large, simple areas.

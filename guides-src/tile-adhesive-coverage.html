There's no single answer, because coverage depends on the trowel, the tile and what you're sticking it to. But the manufacturers publish figures, and they're close enough to plan a job and order the right number of bags.

20kg bag coverage by trowel size

The trowel notch decides how thick the bed is, and that decides how far a bag goes. Mapei's figures for a standard cement-based adhesive:

6mmabout 2.7–3.3 kg6–7.5 m²

 Source: Mapei Adesilex P9 technical data sheet. Adhesive per m² is the bag weight divided by the area.

Walls vs floors

Walls usually take less adhesive than floors, because wall tiles are smaller and lighter and go on with a smaller notch. As an example, a UK retailer listing for BAL Flex One gives these approximate figures for one 20kg bag:

WhereOne 20kg bag coversWorks out at
Dry wallabout 10 m²about 2 kg/m²
Wet wallabout 8 m²about 2.5 kg/m²
Floorabout 5.3 m²about 3.8 kg/m²
 Source: Craven Dunnill listing for BAL Flex One 20kg. The kg/m² column is the bag weight divided by the area.

Wet walls take more because you want full coverage behind every tile, with no voids for water to sit in.

Large-format tiles

Large-format tiles need a thicker, fuller bed, and usually back-buttering too. Mapei gives large-format adhesive use by bed thickness: about 1.2 kg per m² for every mm of bed.

 Worked from Mapei's 1.2 kg/m² per mm figure. Back-buttering adds to this.

Different products vary. A retailer listing for Ultra Tile ProFlex SP, for example, gives 4.5 kg/m² at a 3mm bed, or about 4.4 m² per 20kg bag. Always check the data sheet for the adhesive you're using.

What makes a bag go further or run out sooner

  • Uneven walls and floors soak up far more adhesive. Level or board out first. It's usually cheaper than filling hollows with adhesive.
  • Back-buttering large or heavy tiles adds roughly another millimetre of bed.
  • Textured or ribbed tile backs take more to fill.
  • Solid bed for showers and external floors uses more than a standard notched bed.
  • Mixing more than you can use before it goes off. Rapid-set adhesives give you less working time.

Lift a tile early on to check you're getting full coverage. If you're not, go up a trowel size rather than pressing harder.

Worked examples

Bathroom walls

20 m² of 300×600mm wall tiles in a shower area, at about 2.5–3 kg/m²: 20 × 3 = 60 kg. Order 3 bags, or 4 if the walls are out of true.

Kitchen floor

12 m² of 600×600mm porcelain with a 10mm trowel, at about 5 kg/m²: 12 × 5 = 60 kg. Order 3 bags, plus a fourth if you're back-buttering.

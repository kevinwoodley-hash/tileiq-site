What makes a bag go further or run out sooner

  • Uneven walls and floors soak up far more adhesive. Level or board out first. It's usually cheaper than filling hollows with adhesive.
  • Back-buttering large or heavy tiles adds roughly another millimetre of bed.
  • Textured or ribbed tile backs take more to fill.
  • Solid bed for showers and external floors uses more than a standard notched bed.
  • Mixing more than you can use before it goes off. Rapid-set adhesives give you less working time.

Lift a tile early on to check you're getting full coverage. If you're not, go up a trowel size rather than pressing harder.

Common questions

How much tile adhesive do I need for 10 m²?

For normal size tiles at 2.5–3 kg/m², about 25–30 kg, which is two 20kg bags. Allow a third if the surface is uneven or you're back-buttering. Mosaics need about 20 kg and large tiles about 50 kg.

Do floors need more adhesive than walls?

Usually, yes. Floor tiles are bigger and go down with a larger notch, so expect around 4–5 kg/m² on floors against 2–3 kg/m² on walls.

Does back-buttering use a lot more adhesive?

It adds roughly another millimetre of bed across the tile. On large tiles that's worth an extra bag on most floors.

Can I use these figures for ready-mixed tub adhesive?

No. Ready-mixed tubs are a different product, and their coverage is printed on the tub. Use that figure, or enter it as a custom rate in the calculator.

Why does the calculator give a range instead of an exact amount?

Manufacturers publish a range for normal tiles, and real use moves with the surface, the trowel and how you lay. The range shows what to expect; the bag count rounds up from the top of it so you don't run short.

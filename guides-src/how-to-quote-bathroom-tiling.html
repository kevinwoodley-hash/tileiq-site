Bathrooms are where most tiling quotes go wrong. There are lots of small surfaces, cuts around fittings and prep you can't see until the old tiles come off, and every one of them takes time. This guide follows the order a quote is built in, then works through a real example.

Measure each surface separately

Measure each wall and the floor separately rather than taking one figure for the whole room. Take off doors and windows, but don't deduct small openings: cutting around them takes time and wastes tile. Note which walls are full height and which are half height or splashback only.

Our guide to measuring a room for tiling covers this in detail.

Price the labour

Most tilers price bathroom labour per m², at £20–£50/m² depending on area and tile (see UK tiling labour rates). Then check it against your day rate. Checkatrade gives typical output of 10–15 m² a day on walls and 8–12 m² on floors, but bathrooms are usually at the slow end because of all the cutting.

Charge more per m², or switch to a day rate, for:

  • large-format, natural stone, mosaic or patterned layouts;
  • niches, recesses and boxing;
  • external corners that need mitring or trims;
  • small rooms with lots of cuts.

Price prep and tanking separately

Put these on their own lines so the customer can see where the money goes:

Tanking and wet room prices vary a lot between sources. Price them from your own time and the cost of the kit. Always tank behind tiles in a shower or wet area: tile and grout are not waterproof on their own.

Work out the materials

  • Tiles: the measured area plus 10% for a straight lay, more for diagonal or herringbone. Round up to full boxes. See tile wastage and materials.
  • Adhesive: about 2.5–3 kg per m² for standard tiles and 4–5 kg for large format or floors (Mapei data sheet). Always check the data sheet for the product you're using.
  • Grout, primer, tanking kit, trims and silicone: priced from your merchant.

Say who is supplying the tiles. If the customer is, say so on the quote, and state who pays if they run short.

£1,300 for about 25 m² is roughly £52 per m², close to Checkatrade's average of about £50. Over 4 days it's £325 a day, inside the usual £200–£350 day-rate range. Checking the price both ways like this catches a quote that has drifted too high or too low.

Put it in writing

Send a written quote that lists what's included and excluded, who supplies the tiles, how long the price is valid for and your payment terms. A fixed price that clearly states its exclusions protects you when the old tiles come off and the wall behind them is in poor condition. Our tiling quote template sets out what to include.

The worked example is an illustration, not a price for your area. Use your own rates, your merchant's prices and what you find on site.

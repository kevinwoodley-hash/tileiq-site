Plenty of tilers set their day rate by asking around, then wonder why they're busy all year and still short of money. Work it out from your own figures instead, and you'll know what the lowest rate you can afford really is.

Step 1: Decide what you need to earn

Start with the yearly income you want before tax. That's your wage, not the business's takings. Remember there's no employer adding holiday pay, sick pay or pension contributions, so build those in.

Step 2: Add up your business costs

List everything the business pays for in a year:

  • van: finance or lease, insurance, tax, servicing, tyres;
  • fuel;
  • public liability insurance, and tool cover;
  • tools: replacing and repairing cutters, grinders, mixers, lasers and blades;
  • phone, software and accounting;
  • consumables you don't charge to the customer;
  • advertising and trade memberships.

Keep records. Many of these are allowable expenses that reduce your tax bill. Instead of actual vehicle costs you can use HMRC's simplified mileage rate, which is 55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles from 6 April 2026.

Step 3: Count the days you can actually bill

Days
Weekdays in a year260
Holidays and bank holidays−28
Quoting, surveys, admin, collecting materials−20
Sickness, weather and gaps between jobs−12
Days you can bill200

Your own numbers will differ. If you spend every Friday quoting, you have far fewer billable days than this.

Step 4: Work out your day rate

Add what you want to earn to your business costs, then divide by your billable days.

Worked example

Per year
Income you want before tax£45,000
Insurance, tool replacement, phone and software (typically £800–£1,200)£1,000
Van and fuel: 8,000 business miles × 55p (HMRC simplified rate)£4,400
Total you need to bill£50,400

£50,400 ÷ 200 days = £252 a day. That's within the UK going rate of £200–£350 a day. If your van is on finance, or you drive long distances, put in your actual figures, and your rate will go up. If your figure comes out well above the going rate, look at your costs and how many days you're losing to non-billable work before putting your price up.

Turn your day rate into a per m² rate

Most customers want a price per job, not per day. Divide your day rate by how much you can lay in a day. Checkatrade gives typical output of 10–15 m² on walls and 8–12 m² on floors.

  • £252 ÷ 12 m² a day ≈ £21 per m²
  • £252 ÷ 8 m² a day (bathroom with lots of cuts) ≈ £32 per m²

That's why bathrooms and small rooms need a higher per m² rate than open floors. See UK tiling labour rates to compare.

Don't forget VAT

Once your taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in any rolling 12 months you have to register for VAT and add 20% to your prices for most customers. Keep an eye on your turnover as your rate goes up.

These figures are an example. Use your own costs and talk to an accountant about tax, National Insurance and pension planning.

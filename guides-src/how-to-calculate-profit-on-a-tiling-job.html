Being busy isn't the same as making money. A job can fill your week and still leave you worse off once fuel, the extra bag of adhesive, the trip back for silicone and the day you didn't charge for are counted. Here's how to see what a job actually makes.

What goes into the cost of a job

CostExamples
MaterialsTiles (if you supply them), adhesive, grout, primer, levelling compound, tanking, trims, silicone
Paid labourA labourer or second tiler, subcontractors
Job-specific costsSkip or waste disposal, tool hire, parking, congestion charges, extra travel
Overheads (your share)Van, insurance, phone, software, tool wear: spread over every day you bill
Your own timeEvery day on site, plus the time quoting and collecting materials

The last two are the ones that get forgotten. If your day rate already includes overheads (see working out your day rate), you don't need to add them again. You just need to bill enough days at that rate.

Markup vs margin

Markup is what you add to your cost. Margin is the share of the selling price that's profit. They're not the same number:

Markup on costMargin on price
10%9.1%
15%13.0%
20%16.7%
25%20.0%
33%24.8%

Margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup). If you want a 20% margin on materials, you need a 25% markup.

Worked example

A kitchen floor quoted at £1,600 (no VAT). These are example figures; use your own.

LinePrice to customerYour cost
Labour, 3 days at £300£900—
Tiles, adhesive, grout, levelling (cost £560, 25% markup)£700£560
Skip bag and disposal—£60
Total£1,600£620

£1,600 − £620 = £980 left for your 3 days, or about £327 a day. That's slightly above the £300 day rate you priced at, because the £140 materials markup more than covered the £60 skip.

Now say the job overruns by a day because the floor needed more levelling than expected, and you didn't charge for it. You've still got £980, but it's spread over 4 days, so it drops to £245 a day. If your day rate is set to cover overheads, that day cost you money. This is why:

  • exclusions on the quote matter: "levelling beyond X mm charged at £Y per m²";
  • you should record how many days each job actually took.

Where tiling profit usually leaks

  • Unpriced prep: levelling, removing old adhesive, priming, boarding.
  • Extra trips: a second run to the merchant costs fuel and an hour or two of your day.
  • Over-ordering materials you can't return or use on the next job.
  • Small jobs with no minimum charge. See labour rates.
  • Extras agreed verbally on site and never invoiced.
  • Late payment: money you're owed is money you're lending.

Check the job afterwards

After each job, compare what you quoted with what it actually took: days on site, materials used, extra trips. After a few jobs you'll see which kinds of work pay well and which don't, and you can adjust your rates. Our labour cost calculator shows what a job earns per day before you send the quote.

This guide is about job profitability, not tax. Profit for tax purposes is worked out differently. Talk to an accountant about allowable expenses, and register for VAT if your turnover goes over £90,000.

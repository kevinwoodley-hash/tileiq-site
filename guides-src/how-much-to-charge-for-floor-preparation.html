The tiles only finish as flat as the floor underneath them. Prep is also where most tiling quotes lose money: it's hard to see at the survey, slow to do, and easy to throw in for free. Here's what published guides say it costs, and how to put it on the quote.

Checkatrade also gives a screeding day rate of about £250, or £30–£40 an hour. A 20 kg bag of levelling compound usually covers around 1 m² at 10 mm deep, so compound use rises quickly with depth.

Levelling compound or screed?

  • Levelling compound (a few mm up to the depth the product allows) evens out dips and humps in an otherwise sound floor. It's the most common tiler's prep.
  • Screed (typically 50–75 mm) is for new floors, big level changes or bringing a floor up to height. It's often done by a screeding contractor, and it needs time to dry before you can tile.
  • Boarding timber floors with tile backer board or ply, to give a stiff, stable base. Price the boards, fixings and your time.

How to price prep without losing money

  • Check the floor at the survey with a straightedge or laser, and note where it's out.
  • Price what you can see as a fixed line, for example "level kitchen floor, average 5 mm, 12 m²".
  • Agree a rate for what you can't see. Write it on the quote: "additional levelling over 5 mm charged at £X per m² per mm" or "at day rate".
  • Allow for drying time. If you can't tile until tomorrow, the prep day is a day you can't spend tiling.
Prices are from published UK guides (see Sources) and include regional variation. Always follow the manufacturer's maximum depths and drying times for the product you use.

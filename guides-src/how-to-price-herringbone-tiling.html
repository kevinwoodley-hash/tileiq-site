Herringbone looks great and is in demand, but it's slow. Every row needs cutting at both edges, the setting out has to be exact from the start, and a small error grows across the room. Price it as the specialist job it is.

What makes herringbone slower

  • Setting out: finding the centre line, dry-laying and working out how the pattern meets doorways and the main view of the room.
  • Edge cuts: every row ends in angled cuts at both walls.
  • Accuracy: joints have to stay true across the whole room, so you need to check them more often.
  • Borders: a border or frame around the pattern is extra work.
  • Small tiles: many herringbone tiles are narrow, so there are more tiles and more grout lines per m².

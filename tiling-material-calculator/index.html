How to work out how many tiles you need

You need three things: the area you're tiling, the size of the tile, and how much extra to allow for cuts and breakages.

  1. Measure the area in m². Multiply the length by the width of a floor, or the width by the tiled height of a wall. Split awkward rooms into rectangles and add them up, then take off anything you won't tile.
  2. Add wastage. Multiply the area by 1.10 for 10%, 1.15 for 15%, and so on. This is the tile area to buy.
  3. Divide by the size of one tile to get the number of tiles, or by the m² in a box to get the number of boxes.
  4. Round up to full boxes, and order them all from the same batch so the shade matches.

If you're measuring a room rather than typing in an area, the floor tile calculator works it out from the length and width, and the wall tile calculator takes off doors and windows for you.

How many tiles are in a square metre?

Convert the tile size to metres and multiply, which gives the area of one tile. Then divide 1 by that. A 600 × 300 mm tile is 0.6 × 0.3 = 0.18 m², so there are 1 ÷ 0.18 = 5.56 tiles in a square metre.

How much wastage to allow

Every job loses tile to cuts, breakages and the odd bad tile in a box. These are the usual UK allowances, and they're what the layout setting in the calculator fills in:

LayoutWastage to add
Straight lay (grid or brick bond)10%
Diagonal15%
Herringbone or chevron20–25% (the calculator uses 25%)

Go higher for large-format tiles, small rooms with lots of corners and boxing, patterned tiles that need matching, and fragile tiles like natural stone. The tile wastage guide goes through each case, and there's more on pricing the extra time and tile in how to price herringbone tiling and how to price large-format porcelain.

How much tile adhesive you need

Adhesive depends on the tile and the trowel. Mapei's data sheet for a standard cement-based adhesive (Adesilex P9) gives about 2 kg per m² for mosaics, 2.5–3 kg per m² for standard tiles, and about 5 kg per m² for large-format tiles and external floors. A 20 kg bag covers roughly 6–7.5 m² with a 6 mm notched trowel, or 4–5 m² with a 10 mm one.

So 10 m² of standard wall tiles at 3 kg per m² needs 30 kg, which is two 20 kg bags. Choose the tile type in the calculator to fill in the rate, or type in the figure from your adhesive's data sheet. For coverage by trowel size, worked examples and an adhesive calculator, see how much tile adhesive you need per m².

How much grout you need

Grout depends on the tile size, its thickness and the joint width. The calculator uses the formula most grout manufacturers publish, with all sizes in mm:

(tile length + tile width) ÷ (tile length × tile width) × joint width × tile thickness × 1.6 = kg per m²

For 600 × 600 mm tiles, 9 mm thick with 3 mm joints, that's 1200 ÷ 360,000 × 3 × 9 × 1.6 = 0.144 kg per m². The same joint on 100 × 100 mm tiles uses 0.864 kg per m², six times as much. The calculator adds 10% for waste. Always check the coverage on the bag of the grout you're using.

Frequently asked questions

How many tiles do I need for 10m²?

With 10% wastage for a straight lay, you need to buy 11 m² of tile. That's 31 tiles at 600 × 600 mm, 62 tiles at 600 × 300 mm, or 123 tiles at 300 × 300 mm. Round up to full boxes, and allow more wastage for a diagonal or herringbone layout.

How do I calculate tiles for a bathroom?

Work out the walls and the floor separately, because they usually use different tiles and adhesive. For the walls, add up the width of each wall you're tiling, multiply by the tiled height, and take off the door and window. For the floor, multiply the length by the width and take off anything that sits on an untiled area, like a bath. Add wastage to each, then round each up to full boxes. The wall tile calculator and floor tile calculator do the take-offs for you.

How much extra should I allow for wastage?

Allow 10% for a straight lay, 15% for diagonal and 20–25% for herringbone. Go to the top of the range or above it for large-format tiles, small or awkward rooms, and patterned or fragile tiles.

How many 600 × 600mm tiles are in 1m²?

2.78. One 600 × 600 mm tile covers 0.36 m², and 1 ÷ 0.36 = 2.78. In practice, buy by the box: boxes of 600 × 600 mm tiles usually hold three or four tiles (1.08 or 1.44 m²), so check the label.

How much tile adhesive do I need?

Allow about 3 kg per m² for standard tiles, 2 kg per m² for mosaics and 5 kg per m² for large-format tiles and floors. That means 10 m² of standard tiles needs about 30 kg, or two 20 kg bags. Check the data sheet for the adhesive you're using, as coverage changes with the trowel size.

Why every tiler needs a simple job management system.

25 August 2026 · 5 min read · By Kevin, City & Guilds Level 2 qualified tiler

Running jobs from a notebook, a diary and a few text threads works right up until it doesn't — a double-booked Tuesday, a quote nobody chased, an enquiry that went cold because it got buried under three other conversations. None of it looks like a big problem on its own. All of it adds up.

What goes wrong when tilers don't use a job management system?

Short answer: enquiries go cold, quotes don't get chased, jobs get double-booked and invoices go out late. It's rarely a bad decision. It's that nothing reminds you. The failures are small and quiet:

  • Enquiries go cold. An enquiry that isn't followed up within a day or two often just goes to the next tiler who replies.
  • Nothing gets chased. An unanswered quote or an unpaid invoice relies entirely on you remembering to follow it up — and eventually, one doesn't get remembered.
  • Jobs get double-booked or forgotten. Scheduling from memory or a paper diary makes clashes more likely, not less, especially once you're juggling more than a couple of jobs at once.
  • Customer history lives nowhere. When a past customer calls back, their previous job, quote and notes should be one lookup away — not a search through old messages.

What should a simple tiling job pipeline look like?

Short answer: seven stages — enquiry, surveyed, quoted, accepted, scheduled, in progress, complete — with every job sitting in exactly one of them. A proper system doesn't need to be complicated. It just needs every job to move through the same clear stages, so you always know where each one is up to without holding it in your head.

That's the model behind TileIQ's job management: a single pipeline for every job, and a dashboard that shows what needs attention today — new enquiries, quotes going quiet, and this week's schedule — the moment you open the app.

How do you stop quotes and invoices going quiet?

Short answer: let the system flag them instead of relying on memory. The jobs that get lost usually aren't lost because of a bad decision — they're lost because nothing flagged that a quote had gone quiet, or that an invoice was still unpaid two weeks later.

In TileIQ, a quote that hasn't had a response is flagged for a chase-up after a set number of days — three by default, and you can change it. "I should really follow that up" becomes something the app surfaces for you. When the job is done, the invoice is created from the job, so it can go out the same day.

Where should customer details and job history live?

Short answer: on one customer record, attached to every job you do for them. Customer details, job history and messages shouldn't be scattered across separate text threads and call logs. Save a customer's details once, and reuse them instantly for repeat work — no re-entering an address or a phone number you've already typed in twice before.

You don't need more hours in the day. You need fewer places for a job to quietly fall through.

Does a sole trader really need a system?

Short answer: yes — arguably more than anyone. When you're on your own, there's no office and no one else to remember things. Every enquiry, quote and invoice depends on you, usually while you're on the tools.

A simple pipeline means new enquiries — including ones that come in through a web form or your TileIQ portfolio page — land in the same place as everything else. Nothing has to be typed in twice, and nothing gets missed because it arrived a different way.

Job management for tilers: common questions

Is a spreadsheet good enough to manage tiling jobs?

It can work when you only have a few jobs on. The problem is that a spreadsheet won't remind you to chase a quote, won't build the quote from your measurements, and isn't easy to update from a phone on site. Most tilers outgrow it once several quotes are out at the same time.

What's the difference between job management and a CRM?

A CRM keeps track of customers and conversations. Job management tracks the work itself: measurements, quotes, scheduling and invoicing. For a tiler the two belong together, because every job starts with a customer and every customer comes back as a job.

Do I need it if I only do a few jobs a month?

You'll feel the benefit less than a busy tiler, but the cost of one quote that's never chased, or one invoice that's sent late, is usually bigger than the time it takes to log a job.

What should I record for each tiling job?

At minimum: the customer's details, the measurements, what was quoted and when, whether it was accepted, the booked dates and the invoice status. Photos before and after the job are useful for your own records and for showing your work.
